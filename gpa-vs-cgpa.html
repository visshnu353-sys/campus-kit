GPA vs CGPA: What's the Difference?

GPA and CGPA are related academic measures, but they describe performance over different scopes. The exact definition can vary between universities.

What is GPA?

GPA commonly refers to the grade-point average for a particular term, semester or defined set of courses. It provides a snapshot of performance over that period.

What is CGPA?

CGPA commonly refers to a cumulative grade-point average across multiple terms or courses included under an institution's rules.

Key difference

GPA → a specific academic period or set of courses

CGPA → cumulative performance across the defined academic history

Example

Imagine a student earns a GPA of 8.2 in semester one and 8.8 in semester two. The CGPA is not necessarily the simple average of 8.2 and 8.8 if the semesters have different total credits. A credit-weighted calculation may be required.

Why university rules matter

Grade scales, credit weighting, repeated-course treatment, exclusions and rounding can differ. When preparing an official application, use the calculation method specified by your institution.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is GPA the same as SGPA?

Not always. Many institutions use SGPA for semester-level performance, while GPA can be used more generally. Check your university's terminology.

Is CGPA always the average of semester GPAs?

Not necessarily. If semester credit totals differ, a credit-weighted calculation can produce a different cumulative result.

Can GPA be converted to percentage?

Only using a conversion method recognized by the relevant institution or application.
