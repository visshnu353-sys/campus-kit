How to Calculate CGPA in College

CGPA, or Cumulative Grade Point Average, summarizes your academic performance across subjects or semesters. The exact method can vary by institution, but a credit-weighted calculation is commonly used when subjects carry different credits.

What is CGPA?

CGPA is a grade-point average accumulated across the courses included in a program or the academic period defined by your institution. A subject with more credits can contribute more to a credit-weighted CGPA.

The basic formula

CGPA = Σ(Credit × Grade Point) ÷ Σ(Credits)

For each subject, multiply its credit value by the grade point earned. Add those weighted values and divide by the total number of credits.

Worked example

Example:
Mathematics: 4 credits × 9 points = 36
Programming: 3 × 8 = 24
Networks: 3 × 9 = 27
Database Systems: 4 × 8 = 32

Total weighted points = 119
Total credits = 14
CGPA = 119 ÷ 14 = 8.50

Step-by-step method

  1. List every subject included in the calculation.
  2. Write the credit value for each subject.
  3. Convert each grade into the grade point used by your institution.
  4. Multiply credits by grade points.
  5. Add all weighted points.
  6. Divide by total credits.
  7. Round only according to your institution's stated rule.

CGPA is not always the same as an average of subject grades

If subjects have different credits, simply adding grade points and dividing by the number of subjects can produce a different result. Credit weighting accounts for the academic load of each course.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I use credits in CGPA calculation?

If your university defines CGPA using credit-weighted grade points, yes. Always follow the grading rules published by your institution.

How many decimals should CGPA have?

Use the precision specified by your university or academic portal. Avoid changing the displayed value just to make it look higher.

Can I calculate CGPA from percentage?

Only if your institution provides a recognized conversion rule. There is no single universal percentage-to-CGPA formula for every university.
