How to Calculate Internal Marks

Internal assessment can combine several components such as tests, assignments, laboratory work, quizzes and attendance. The important part is understanding the maximum marks and weight assigned to each component.

Weighted marks formula

Weighted Score = (Obtained ÷ Maximum) × Weight

Add the weighted scores of all components to obtain the total weighted internal score.

Worked example

Example:
Test: 36/50 with weight 20 → 14.4
Assignment: 18/20 with weight 10 → 9
Lab: 27/30 with weight 20 → 18

Total internal score = 41.4 / 50

Step-by-step method

  1. Write every assessment component.
  2. Record obtained and maximum marks.
  3. Record the official weight or converted maximum.
  4. Calculate each component's weighted contribution.
  5. Add the contributions.
  6. Check the result against the official assessment scheme.

Why raw marks can be misleading

A 36/50 score and an 18/20 score are not directly added when their components carry different weights. Converting each component to its assigned weight makes the contributions comparable.

Check your syllabus or faculty instructions

Different courses can use different assessment structures. If your course uses a special normalization, best-of-two rule or replacement policy, apply that rule before calculating the final internal score.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I just add all marks together?

Only when all components use the same maximum scale and no weighting or normalization rule applies.

What if my college takes the best two tests?

Apply the official best-of-two rule before calculating the weighted component.

Why does my calculation differ from the portal?

Check for normalization, rounding, attendance components, dropped assessments or special course rules.
