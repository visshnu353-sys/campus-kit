How to Calculate Attendance Percentage

Attendance percentage tells you what share of conducted classes you attended. Knowing the formula also lets you estimate the effect of attending or missing future classes.

Attendance formula

Attendance % = (Classes Attended ÷ Classes Conducted) × 100

Worked example

Example: If you attended 42 out of 50 conducted classes:

Attendance = (42 ÷ 50) × 100 = 84%.

How future classes change your percentage

Your percentage changes after every new conducted class. If you attend the next class, both attended and conducted counts increase by one. If you miss it, only conducted classes increase.

Finding classes needed for a target

Suppose you currently have A attended classes out of C conducted classes and want a target T. If you attend every next class, find the smallest n such that (A+n)/(C+n) × 100 is at least T. CampusKit's attendance calculator can perform this what-if calculation for you.

Keep subject-wise attendance separate

Many colleges apply attendance rules per subject. An overall attendance percentage can hide a low-attendance subject, so record attendance separately when your institution evaluates each course independently.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does attendance percentage round up?

That depends on your institution's rule. Your academic portal or attendance policy is the authoritative source.

Should I calculate attendance for every subject?

Yes, when attendance requirements are subject-specific.

Can I know how many classes I can miss?

A what-if calculation can estimate this, but policies, future timetable changes and rounding rules can affect the actual result.
